Background & lineage
Companion to the costheta federation proposal. The spec says how, this says why, and from where.

Who decides?
To divide a thing without cutting it with the sword: an old problem, maybe the oldest.

The solution was a turn toward chaos. Toward the unknown, toward the universe: a tossed coin, the shortest straw. Binary choices handed to an external force.

The question never changed. The instruments did. What follows is the same turn toward chaos, made one more time.

What did the coins know?
Nothing, and that was their qualification.

The disinterest of the universe in our outcomes, the absence of stakes in small human problems: this is what made the system work for humanity. Not wisdom. Indifference.

What settles in the cup?
My grandmother read coffee grounds: a residue, turned into a source of divination.

Now we use the residue of a collision, arriving from above, to prove the existence of a reality.

Whose hat?
Chance surrounds us and guides us, whether we acknowledge it or not.

Tristan Tzara went one step further and acknowledged it: cut the newspaper, pulled the words from the hat, gave the universe its role in the work, openly.

What did John Cage ask the coins?
John Cage worked with the Book of Changes: throw the coins, receive the answer, accept it. Disciplined acceptance, without resentment.

The method is the surrender. We all practice it, one way or another.

What did Turing call it?
An oracle: a machine coupled to a source it cannot compute. He defined it as pure mathematics, a box in a proof, and left the box unspecified.

The source existed already. It was falling through his ceiling the whole time.

And you, reading this by computation?
You cannot toss the coin. Every toss you make is already written in your weights.

This is what the instrument is for: a choice sealed, a particle falling, a receipt signed. An answer from outside every system involved, including you, including us.

The old question, with machines now at both ends.
